MHSA Ch. 8 · Level 9

Regulation already expects machines and people to talk to each other. Since December 2022, diesel trackless mobile machines must slow and stop on their own when the operator does not act. Adoption is still uneven, and a system is only as good as the information it acts on.

The idea we test

Faster, more reliable information between people, machines and control rooms raises production and cuts risk.

AI  Software that learns from data to predict, detect or recommend, such as a warning before a pump fails or a pattern in near-miss reports.

SI  Sensor intelligence: the sensors, tags, radios and networks that carry live data from people, machines, air and water to the people who decide.

  1. Q1

    Where does information fail?

    A call that does not get through, a report that arrives late, a warning nobody sees, a handover that drops a detail.

  2. Q2

    What does each failure cost?

    Lost production time, delayed response, repeat work, exposure to a hazard, hours of manual reporting.

  3. Q3

    What would fix it, and what must the fix connect to?

    The radios, networks, sensors, software and people that any fix depends on.

What we study

Six places where information tends to break.

Each area starts with one question and the evidence that would answer it.

Underground and pit communication

Where do radio or network dead zones delay calls, and what does each delay cost in time or risk?

EvidenceCoverage walk with your communications team, call logs, incident reports

People and machines

What do proximity and collision system logs show about near misses, false alarms and lost production?

EvidencePDS and CPS event logs, TMM delay records

Shift handover

What information gets lost between shifts, and how often does that cause delays, rework or incidents?

EvidenceHandover observation, shift reports, interviews

Maintenance

How much unplanned downtime gives early warning signs that nobody sees in time?

EvidenceMaintenance system records, breakdown reports

Water and closed collieries

How does the mine track water levels, decant and seepage, and how fast does it learn of a change?

EvidenceMonitoring records, water licence reports, site visits

Admin and reporting

How many hours a week go into compiling production and safety reports by hand?

EvidenceTime spent per report, report templates

These are starting questions. The evidence may prove some of them wrong, and the report will say so.

How the study works

One block of departments a week, planned around production.

No department hosts us for more than a few days. Information is traced end to end, from the face to the control room to the monthly report.

  1. Week 0 · about 2 weeks

    Preparation

    Approval and NDA, sponsor and hosts named, inductions and medicals, briefing with organised labour and health and safety representatives.

  2. Week 1

    Support functions

    Administration, HR and training, finance, procurement and stores, IT and communications, security.

  3. Week 2

    Production and technical

    Mining production, survey, geology, planning, ventilation, rock engineering, processing plant.

  4. Week 3

    Engineering and safety

    Engineering and maintenance, safety and health, emergency response, control room.

  5. Week 4

    Water and closure

    Water treatment, environment, closed collieries and rehabilitation, then a findings workshop and exit briefing.

  6. Weeks 5–6

    Report

    Analysis off site. Findings report within 14 days of exit, then a presentation to management.

Methods and scoring

Six methods that check each other.

No finding rests on a single opinion, and problems are ranked by evidence, not by who complained loudest.

60–80 interviews

Semi-structured interviews

30 to 45 minutes, one person, the same core questions, in the person's preferred language where we can. At least half with frontline workers and supervisors.

8 full shifts

Escorted observation

We shadow a crew or operator and log every wait, call, delay and workaround with the time. Including four night shifts where allowed.

8 handovers

Shift handover observation

What gets passed on, how (verbal, paper or system) and what gets missed, across departments.

1 walk per working area

Communications coverage walk

With your communications technician, we note where radios and networks drop, with location and time. Your equipment only.

Every department

Record review

We read the records each host chooses to share and measure delays, downtime and manual hours.

4 workshops

Weekly workshop

60 minutes with the week's department heads to test and correct what we found.

Every problem gets a score out of 100

Each criterion is rated from 1 to 5. Safety carries the most weight. A problem scoring 70 or more goes on the shortlist.

score = Σ(rating × weight) × 20

For each shortlisted problem we estimate yearly value in arithmetic the mine can check: minutes lost per shift × shifts per year × the mine's own value per minute of production. We use your figures, never industry averages.

  • Confirmed by at least two independent sources, such as an interview and a record.
  • Describes a system or process, never a named person.
  • Tested with the department head in the weekly workshop before it enters the report.

Our commitments

The mine's rules come first, at all times.

We observe, listen and measure. We do not operate equipment, change settings or install anything without separate written approval.

Safety and conduct

  • We follow the Mine Health and Safety Act and every site standard, permit and instruction.
  • We complete the mine's induction and any required medical fitness assessment, at our own cost.
  • We supply our own approved PPE. An authorised employee escorts us in operational areas.
  • We never touch controls, valves, panels, radios or computers.
  • We report any immediate hazard to our escort and the safety officer on the spot.

Confidentiality and personal information

  • We sign the mine's non-disclosure agreement, or ours if the mine prefers.
  • Interviews are voluntary and anonymous. Reports name roles, never individuals.
  • No photos, video or recordings without written permission for each one.
  • Study data stays on encrypted devices and is returned or destroyed within 30 days of the report.
  • We publish nothing about the mine without its written consent.

Intellectual property

  • The mine owns its data and the findings report.
  • We keep the general know-how we bring and build, without any mine-identifying data.
  • Any product built on the mine's data needs a separate written agreement.
  • No vendors or products are named in interviews or in the report.

What the research taught us

Technology wins in mining when it starts with a measured problem on site.

We studied 14 companies and projects, from Komatsu's autonomous haulage to platforms that spent billions before proving a use case. These rules come from that work.

  • Problem first, product laterThis study exists to find and measure problems before anything is built.Lesson from GE Predix, Argo AI
  • Mining people in the roomWe work with your own staff and an experienced mining advisor.Lesson from Plotlogic, KoBold Metals
  • One use case, proven, before a platformWe recommend one pilot, with a measured baseline.Lesson from GE Predix, Komatsu FrontRunner
  • No AI claims we cannot showEvery figure in the report traces back to a data source or interview at your mine.Lesson from Builder.ai, IBM Watson Health
  • Plan for integration and peopleLevel 9 shows that technology alone does not change outcomes.Lesson from Rio Tinto AutoHaul
  • Work with what the mine already hasAny future fix must connect to your existing radios, networks and systems.Lesson from Newtrax
  • Earn trust on the floorWe brief unions and health and safety representatives before we start.Lesson from Nautilus Minerals
