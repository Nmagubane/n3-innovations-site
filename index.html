Regulation already expects machines and people to communicate. Since December 2022, diesel trackless mobile machines must slow down and stop on their own when the operator does not act. A system like that is only as good as the information it acts on.

The problem we solve

Mines already collect the warnings. Too often, they do not reach the right person in time.

Condition-monitoring reports, alarm logs, maintenance records and shift reports can point to a failure before it happens. Each sits in a different system, inbox or spreadsheet, so by the time someone connects them, the pump has failed or the shift has ended.

Each large vendor owns one source of data: the machines, the sensors, the historian or the work-order system. Nobody neutral owns the step between "a warning exists" and "the right job is done on time". That step is where we work.

Methods and scoring

Six methods that check each other, and a score out of 100 for every problem.

60 to 80 interviews, eight full shifts observed (four at night where allowed), eight shift handovers, a communications coverage walk in each main working area, a review of the records each department chooses to share, and a weekly workshop with department heads.

Each criterion is rated from 1 to 5, and safety carries the most weight. A problem that scores 70 or more goes on the shortlist.

score = Σ(rating × weight) × 20

For each shortlisted problem, we estimate the yearly value with arithmetic the mine can check: minutes lost per shift × shifts per year × the mine's own value per minute of production. We use the mine's figures, never industry averages.

A finding enters the report only when two independent sources confirm it, it describes a system rather than a person, and the department head has tested it in the weekly workshop.

  • Safety risk30%Can this failure contribute to an injury or a fatality?
  • Production impact25%Minutes or tonnes lost per shift
  • Frequency15%Daily, weekly, monthly or rare
  • Data available10%Does the data a fix needs already exist at the mine?
  • Ease of fixing10%Time and cost to fix, scored so that easy fixes score high
  • Fit with existing systems10%Can a fix connect to the radios, networks and software already in use?

Our commitments

The mine's rules come first, at all times.

Safety and conduct

  • We follow the Mine Health and Safety Act, 1996, and every site standard, permit and instruction.
  • We complete the mine's induction and any required medical fitness assessment, at our own cost.
  • We wear our own approved PPE, with an authorised escort in all operational areas.
  • We observe only. We never operate, adjust or connect to any equipment or system.
  • We report any immediate hazard to our escort and the safety officer at once.

Confidentiality and POPIA

  • We sign the mine's non-disclosure agreement, or ours if the mine prefers.
  • Interviews are voluntary and anonymous. Reports name roles, never individuals.
  • We take no photos, video or recordings without written permission for each one.
  • Study data stays on encrypted devices and is returned or destroyed within 30 days of the report.
  • We publish nothing about the mine without its written consent.

Intellectual property

  • The mine owns its data and the findings report.
  • We keep the general know-how we bring and build, without any data that identifies the mine.
  • Any product built on the mine's data needs a separate written agreement.
  • We name no vendors or products in interviews or in the report.

The full proposal and the study methodology are available on request. They set out each day, each method and each template, along with insurance, indemnity and approval steps.
